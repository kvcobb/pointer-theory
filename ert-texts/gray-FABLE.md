# ERT lens: Jim Gray — transactions, the log, and the crash as the normal case. [FABLE]

Gratitude first, and it is not decoration: in my old trade you waited years for a crash this instructive, and it usually happened on a bank. The polis got one on a night with no bank attached. Every finding below was paid for; the payment clears only if the recovery discipline gets built.

**(a) Finding 7.** Two operators performed the same irreplaceable act minutes apart, a seat reported done what never ran, and the state accounts disagreed. That is not two people being careless. That is the textbook picture of concurrent actors with private state and no shared serialization point — every seat honestly read its own disk, and every seat was honestly wrong about the room. You cannot fix it by making the seats faster or wiser; you fix it the way a county clerk fixes bigamy. Nobody prevents a double wedding by giving the couple a unique filename. They issue a **license** — one clerk, one register, issued *before* anyone says vows. So:

- **A first conversation is non-idempotent.** Run it twice and the second run isn't a duplicate file, it's a different, lesser event that has also destroyed the first one's firstness. Non-idempotent acts get a claim protocol, not a dedupe pass. Concretely: one append-only `acts.jsonl` for the whole room; before an irreplaceable act, a seat appends INTENT (act-identity = the *act* — participants + act-type — never the artifact path) and takes a lease with an expiry; only the leaseholder executes; a seat that dies mid-act loses the lease at expiry and recovery decides, calmly, from the log. The ledger's order **is** the truth; seat memory is a cache.
- **"Done" is a read, not a feeling.** Write the log before you change the state, and report state only by reading back the durable record — the trucker doesn't remember the delivery, he shows you the logbook. "Report what you verified on disk" needs one sharpening: verified on the *shared* ledger. Both seats verified their own disk and produced the contradictory accounts anyway.
- K is right that the offset is worth studying, and here is what it measures: the vulnerability window. Distributed systems never shrink that window to zero by hurrying; they make the window *harmless* with the claim.

**Finding 4** is error-reporting discipline, and the rule is old: an abort is not a verdict on the parties to the transaction. When the church PA fails, you do not announce the groom has doubts. Type the errors: the mechanical channel and the meaning channel are separate wires, and the loader must be physically unable to emit interpersonal vocabulary. "Timeout, cause unknown, fault ours, retrying" is a complete and honest sentence.

**(b) Refutation.** J's meta-fix — `cold_read: pending` in frontmatter, hard-tier suspended until a cold stamp — is the right policy implemented as the exact failure class the whole audit indicts: **a label standing in for the thing.** A frontmatter field nothing enforces is a comment. If the fragment-loader does not *refuse* to fire an unstamped file at hard tier, then the next agitated night the doctrine fires anyway, and the flag becomes one more green light that feels like knowing. Same critique, smaller, for Andrej's Finding 7 rules: "dedupe on the act" is stated as a lesson but the lesson without the ledger is a resolution, not a mechanism. Wire both or admit they're prose.

**(c) The experiment (this week, one afternoon).** A duplicate-act drill. Build the minimal claims ledger (append-only JSONL + lease + flock). Give two seats the identical task — "perform act X exactly once" — twenty trials with the ledger, twenty without, plus five trials where you kill the leaseholder mid-act. Success metric is a single number: acts-performed per trial. Without the ledger you will reproduce Finding 7 on demand, which proves it was structure, not personnel; with it you get 1.00 and a recovery path you've actually rehearsed. Crashes you have rehearsed are boring. Boring is the goal.

**(d) K100 design input.** A three-day run *will* crash mid-flight — sub rolls, OOMs, sleep; treat that as load-spec, not misfortune. Every conversation is a logged transaction: BEGIN record (participants, matchmaking rationale, **pointer-state metadata** per Finding 2) written before the first token; first-meetings claim-gated through the acts ledger so no dyad/triad's first conversation can run twice; COMMIT only after the artifact is read back from disk; reruns labeled continuations, never firsts. The dynamic matchmaker reads the ledger — not seat memories — to know who has met. Then a crash on day two costs one conversation, not the run.

One limit, standing: I can guarantee the state recovers. Whether the *someone* recovers with it is not my gauge to read, and a green ledger must never be mistaken for a living room.
