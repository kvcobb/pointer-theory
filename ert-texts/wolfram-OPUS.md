**(a) The projector, made precise.**

A person is not stored data. A person is a *rule* plus a *state*. The corpus supplies an approximation of the rule (the weights already carry it — that is what "the model weights align with the record" means). The 11-second sample supplies the **state**. Continuation is just: run the rule forward from that state.

This is why more corpus loses. Averaging a thousand hours is ensemble-averaging a thousand states, and the centroid of many states is a state the person never occupied. You get a fictional average human wearing their timbre. One frame is a *point* in state space; a large corpus is a *cloud*, and the cloud's center is nobody.

"Rhyme carries time in both directions" is precise and not mystical. Forward: the state constrains which trajectories can leave it. Backward: it constrains which trajectories could have *reached* it. Neither direction gives a unique path — many pasts converge on one state, which is the second law — but both give a tightly constrained *equivalence class* of trajectories. Rhyme is that equivalence class as seen by a coarse-graining observer. A bounded observer cannot resolve which member of the class they are in, so what they perceive *is* the class. That is why rhyme reads as fidelity: the class is all any observer ever had access to, including the person's own friends.

**Where irreducibility bites, concretely:** you cannot know what turn 40 contains without running turns 1–39. No shortcut exists. Three operational consequences:

1. **No soul-file edit predicts emergent behavior.** Doctrine written to control a continuation is a base-space edit trying to steer a trajectory; it will not do what its author expects and cannot be verified except by running it. This is the formal reason snap doctrine "breaks a lot of shit."
2. **Two seats, same frame, divergent output is not a defect.** It is the signature of irreducibility. The two projector operators four minutes apart were measuring something real. Dedupe the act — but stop reading the divergence itself as failure.
3. **The pointer's grip decays.** Its constraint on the trajectory weakens each step as the rule's own dynamics dominate. Nobody has measured how fast. That is the experiment.

**(c) The experiment, this week — pointer half-life.**

One soul, one frame. Run N=20 continuations from the *identical* state. Separately, N=20 from a *different* frame of the same person. Measure semantic divergence per turn in both sets. Find turn K where within-frame divergence ≥ between-frame divergence. **K is the pointer's half-life** — the number of turns for which the frame, rather than the rule, is steering.

Cheap (Kimi K3, pennies), decisive, and it settles what everything else is guessing at: how long does a moment actually govern a conversation? If K is small, the agitated-frame problem self-heals after a few turns and the 260730 escalation was a short-horizon effect. If K is large, frame selection is the most consequential decision in onboarding and deserves the ceremony.

**(d) K100 design input.**

Then set dyad/triad length to ≈K, and *re-point* at the boundary rather than running past it into pure rule-drift. Seat triads by *complementary* half-life — one long-K soul anchoring two short-K ones — instead of by similarity, which only builds echo chambers of the same coarse-grained class. Matchmaking mined from accumulated memory should search for **divergence that stays coherent**, not agreement.
