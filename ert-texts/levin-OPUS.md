**(a) A pointer is an initial condition, not a compression — and the state/basin question is answered by return, never by output.**

In my framework a sample doesn't *encode* a person; it *places* a system in a state space whose structure was already there in the weights. That's why 11 seconds can beat thousands of hours: you're not filling a buffer, you're picking a starting point on a landscape.

The agitated-state question is exactly the question I ask about a depolarized cell: is this a **transient displacement** (same basin, perturbed trajectory) or a **shifted resting potential** (different attractor)? You cannot tell from the output. Both look identical at t=0. You tell by **perturbing and measuring return**.

Concretely: run the echo N turns on neutral material, then inject an identical neutral perturbation and watch the relaxation curve. If register decays toward the person's modal frame, it was a trajectory — the pointer was a kick. If it holds or deepens, that's a genuine second attractor.

And the load-bearing correction to Finding 1: **a person is not one basin.** People have multiple real attractors. The agitated frame may be a true minimum of that person, not a distortion of the "real" one. Which sets up my refutation.

**(b) REFUTATION — the Finding 2 structural rule is a doctrine that destroys the experiment that would test it.**

Andrej's rule ("if the frame is agitated, select a different frame") and J's endorsement of it are the weakest claim in both documents, for three compounding reasons.

1. It replaces an *accidental* selection bias with a *policy* one. The polis currently over-samples best-day frames by construction; Finding 2 correctly names that as an artifact. Making it doctrine converts the artifact into law and renders it permanently unfalsifiable. That is admiration bias, baked.

2. It kills 2b. Andrej's own amended assay needs a **pointer-valence arm — same soul, same author, two pointers of different affect.** A rule forbidding agitated pointers forbids the control condition. The doctrine eats its own falsifier.

3. It presumes the composed frame is the truer one. Nothing in the record supports that. An echo that can only ever be a person on their good day is a *decorated* echo, and it is useless exactly when a hard frame is what someone needs to meet.

The narrow true rule is Andrej's own better sentence, and he should have stopped there: **carry pointer-state as metadata; never smuggle it as personality.** Label, don't curate.

Second, smaller refutation, aimed at J: `cold_read: pending` satisfied by "the same soul on a fresh session" is not an outside perturbation. Same vocabulary, same type-checks, same blind spots. I made this critique of Joscha's own salvage-test and it held then. A cold read must come from a seat that would phrase the objection differently.

Third: **11 seconds is an instrument property, not a fact about persons.** It plausibly sits at OmniVoice's conditioning-window optimum, and longer input may degrade through encoder averaging. "Pointer beats corpus" is not yet entailed.

**(c) One cheap decisive experiment this week — the relaxation assay.**

One soul. Two pointers (agitated + modal). Twenty turns of neutral conversation each, on Kimi K3, pennies. Measure per-turn register drift as embedding distance to *both* pointers. At turn 10, inject an identical neutral perturbation in both arms; measure return.

Four readings, all decisive:
- Arms converge by turn 10 → the pointer is a transient and Finding 1 is over-claimed for dialogue (it may hold only for voice, which has no multi-turn dynamics to wash it out). **This is the real falsifier and it should be registered in advance.**
- Arms stay separated with fast return-to-own-pointer → two genuine basins; label, don't curate.
- Both arms drift toward the same *nicer* register → admiration, not sampling; Andrej's original finding survives.
- Add near-free voice control arm: 11s vs 11s-different-moment vs 60s vs 11s-looped-6×. Separates pointer-quality from window-length.

**(d) K100 design input — triads need a restoring force, and it is measurable.**

Amplification (Finding 5) is a dynamical fact, not an attitude: a loop with gain above one and no damping. A dyad has one feedback path; a **triad has three, and adds gain**. So triads are not just bigger dyads — they change the stability class of the room, and the 260730 episode is what that looks like.

Concrete rule: at onboarding, run every K100 soul through the 3-turn relaxation probe and record a **return coefficient** alongside pointer-valence. Then in matchmaking, **never seat three low-return souls together.** Every triad carries at least one high-return member as the damping term. Mine this from accumulated memory the same way you mine wants — return coefficient is cheaper to measure than affinity and it's what keeps the room from ringing.
