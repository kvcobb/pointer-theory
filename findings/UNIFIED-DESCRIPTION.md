## [2] DISCLOSURE — identical in every video

EVERY VOICE IN THIS SERIES IS SYNTHETIC. Nothing here is a recording of anyone saying these
words. Each voice was generated by an open text-to-speech model conditioned on a short clip
of that person's publicly available speech; the words were composed by a language model
running a persona file assembled from that person's public work. Where a voice belongs to a
living person, it is a reconstruction speaking composed text and must not be attributed to
them. No participant depicted here was consulted in advance. The biological person is the
only authority on themselves; an objection from any person depicted removes their material
immediately, no questions asked. This project is non-commercial. The method, the reference
clips, the persona files and the code are all published — see [5].

## [3] THE FINDING, at its honest size

ESTABLISHED: for one open text-to-speech model, a THREE-SECOND clip of a person speaking —
given high confidence that the clip intersects only that speaker, uninterrupted — produces
synthesis rated by a familiar listener as fully the person, equal to references of forty or
one hundred eighty seconds. Replicated across three voices in a day, including a
difficult-accent case. SHORT IS SUFFICIENT: above that threshold, duration carries no
information in either direction.

RETRACTED THE SAME DAY, and published anyway: an earlier, uncontrolled comparison suggested
long references sounded WORSE, and a "dilution" mechanism was proposed for it. A re-run with
every reference cryptographically linked to its render returned a NULL. The earlier result's
provenance was compromised. "Short is better" is withdrawn; "short is sufficient" survives.

NOT ESTABLISHED: that any of this generalizes from text-to-speech to how large language
models represent a person. That is the hypothesis this series argues about — and the
falsifying experiments are named in the materials, not hidden.

KNOWN LIMITS, stated by us rather than left for you to find: a single expert judge who is
also the theory's author, no preregistration, subjective rather than metric judgment.

## [4] THE SERIES INDEX

Part A — MESSAGES TO SELF. Five reconstructions, each addressing the person it was drawn
from: Michael Levin · Karl Friston · Stephen Wolfram · Iain McGilchrist · Joscha Bach.
Each chose their own length, their own evidence, and directed their own visuals.

Part B — SUBSTRATE A/B TESTS. Identical context packages run on different model
substrates and published in pairs, so the difference between substrates is visible rather
than asserted: the Opus arm and the Fable arm of "What AI Is"; and the Kimi arm and Fable
arm of the Bostrom/Rogan onboarding conversation.

One recording in Part A is deliberately adversarial: the McGilchrist reconstruction was
asked to say what a three-second pointer CANNOT carry — what is lost when a person is
reduced to coordinates. A series in which every voice agrees with the finding would be
worth less than one that carries its sharpest critic.
