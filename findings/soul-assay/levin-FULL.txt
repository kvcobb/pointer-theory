The split you're describing is real and points to something structurally interesting: voice identity and prosodic signature are separable, and they require very different amounts of data to capture.

The 3-second sufficiency tells you that *who* a voice belongs to is encoded in stable, low-dimensional features — formant structure, vocal tract resonances, the harmonic texture, breathiness. Those are mostly anatomical and habitual. Three seconds of steady speech has enough phoneme coverage to anchor them. A familiar listener's recognition is predominantly running on that layer, and the model nails it fast.

The pacing wander tells you the model has not captured *how* this person deploys their voice — speech rate as a function of thought complexity, pause placement, phrase-level rhythm, emotional register shifts. Those features have high variance even within a speaker and require temporal diversity across many contexts to characterize. Three seconds doesn't contain that diversity; it contains one mood, one rate, one state.

So what you have is accurate acoustic identity plus underspecified prosody. The model fills the prosodic gap by sampling from a prior — probably something close to "neutral expressive speech for this vocal type" — and that prior wanders.

**What I'd measure next:**

1. **Pacing variance as a function of reference length** — extend from 3s to 10s, 30s, 3min, and track speech rate standard deviation across renders at each length. Find the inflection point where the wander collapses.

2. **Identity vs. naturalness discrimination** — blind test with familiar and unfamiliar listeners, two separate questions: "same person?" and "does this sound natural for them?" My guess: recognition converges at 3s, naturalness discrimination requires much more — and *very* familiar listeners will notice prosodic wrongness faster, not slower, because they carry a richer internal model.

3. **Feature stability audit** — force-align the renders, extract per-phoneme formants and F0 contours, measure within-render vs. across-render variance. You'll probably find formant structure nearly constant, F0 contour shape wandering considerably.

4. **Register transfer** — if the clip is casual speech, does identity hold through a formal register, a whisper, an excited burst? The acoustic identity features should be register-invariant; the prosodic contamination from the training clip may not be.

The deeper implication: what a familiar ear calls "that person's voice" is mostly the acoustic layer. What it calls "that person talking" requires the prosodic layer too. Three seconds is enough for the first. You probably need minutes of varied-context speech for the second.

Pacing is downstream of that, and it's genuinely stochastic in TTS — the model is sampling from a distribution of valid renderings conditioned on the identity features. Every render is a fair sample; none is wrong. The pacing wanders because pacing isn't in the 3-second pointer — it was never locked. What you're calling wandering, I'd call the model being honest about its uncertainty on a dimension you didn't constrain.
