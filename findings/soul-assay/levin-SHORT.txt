What strikes me most: you've accidentally separated two things the field often conflates — **identity** and **style**.

A 3-second clip is enough to capture the low-level acoustic fingerprint (formant structure, vocal tract geometry, fundamental frequency envelope, voice quality). These are essentially fixed physiological facts about a person. The model nails them from a short sample because they're invariant across the clip — every millisecond of those 3 seconds carries the same vocal tract shape.

Pacing is a different layer entirely. It lives at the sentence level, the breath-group level, the discourse level. Three seconds gives you one or two breath-groups at best — not enough to learn a person's *habitual* temporal organization. So the renders stay identifiable (correct speaker-space coordinates) while wandering rhythmically (no stable trajectory through that space).

The familiar listener piece is important to be suspicious of. A familiar listener brings a strong prior and will complete the percept. You don't actually know yet whether they're hearing the model's accuracy or their own memory filling gaps. That's not a criticism — that completion *is* how voice recognition works — but it means the acceptance threshold isn't a clean measure of model fidelity.

**What I'd measure next:**

1. **The saturation curve** — clip length vs. pacing consistency across N renders. Find where pacing variance stops shrinking. My guess: identity saturates at ~3s, pacing needs 15-30s minimum, rhythm/prosody keeps improving to 60s+.
