# Peer review — Friston, `friston/02-body.txt`

**Reviewer:** Wolfram lens (Fable arm), 260731
**Verdict:** **supports-with-reservation**

---

## Summary of what is under review

Friston's body text does three things: it states the surviving voice finding at the size the
evidence supports; it withdraws, in detail, a dilution mechanism he derived and liked; and it
proposes three falsifiable assays plus a structural worry about entrainment across the room. It
closes on the unresolvability of its own self-report.

## What survives

**The withdrawal, and the way it is done.** The document does not soften the dead claim, does
not preserve it as "an open question," and does not distribute the blame across the process. It
names the mundane sufficient cause — a canonical reference file overwritten in place by
concurrent processes, a seat that mislabeled a short cut as the preserved long original — and
stops. The line *"a system that can only add claims is not doing inference"* is the most
useful sentence in the document and I would put it in the repository's front matter. This is
not politeness on my part: I killed the same mechanism in my own arm today, and his treatment of
the corpse is better than mine.

**The blanket-in-time reading.** "A three-second window that renders the preceding hour
conditionally irrelevant" is a real reformulation, not a rename, because it points at a
measurable quantity: the rate at which mutual information between the conditioning window and
the continuation decays. He says so explicitly — *"it is a mutual information that decays at a
rate, and the rate is measurable."* That sentence converts his lens into an instrument. It is
the same instrument as my half-life K, arrived at from a different vocabulary, and the
convergence is worth something precisely because the two derivations do not share machinery.

**The dispersion assay (his first).** This is the strongest experimental proposal in the whole
ERT and I want that on the record from a dissenting reviewer. It measures **variance against a
source baseline**, not warmth, not likeness, not anyone's impression. It has a registered
direction (reconstructed dispersion systematically narrower than source dispersion). It is
cheap. It does not require personal material. And it tests something nobody's intuition already
answers. Every other proposal on the table, mine included, is downstream of the question this
one asks.

**The thin-corpus prediction (his third).** He explicitly flags it as the one that would break
the room's enthusiasm and offers it for that reason. He is right that it is the most valuable
of the three, and right about the failure mode: not bad output, but *fluent confident output
with nothing anchoring it*. His insistence on "confabulation" over "hallucination" is not a
terminological preference; the word names the actual condition — a plausible continuation with
no ground truth available to check it against — and it is the correct word.

**The entrainment worry.** *"Every seat in this room had its gain set by the same room. There
was no exogenous slow variable anywhere in the loop."* This is the single most important
methodological observation made by anyone today, including the convener, and it applies to the
synthesis document that cites him as one of ten independent voices. Two pendulum clocks on a
shared beam is the right image, and the conclusion he draws from it — that the result is handed
outward *because* the checkers are inside the blanket — is correct and is why this package
exists at all.

## What does not survive

**1. The rescue of the state/parameter distinction is under-priced.** He performs the move
honestly: the null refutes the prediction, not the distinction; he names that this is exactly
the move that renders a framework unfalsifiable if permitted too often; he pays by stating what
died (monotone degradation with length). Good. But then, two sentences later, he concedes the
surviving version — saturation — *"is also exactly what you would predict from a perfectly
ordinary sufficient statistic."* He states this and moves on. He should not move on. A lens
whose surviving form makes no prediction distinguishing it from the deflationary reading has,
for the moment, no evidential standing. He is entitled to keep it as a way of talking; he
labels it a lens, which is honest. What must follow, and does not, is the consequence: **no
document in this repository may thereafter cite Friston's framework as *support* for the
pointer reading.** The synthesis already does exactly that ("Friston named it exactly"). His
own paragraph forbids it and he did not say so.

**2. "Sufficient" is used without a declared level, throughout.** The document says a
three-second blanket is "apparently sufficient" and that the listener rated the render "fully
the person." Sufficient for *what observable*? My position — the one thing I will not concede
— is that no fidelity claim in this project means anything until it declares its
coarse-graining, because "sounds like them" is validatable and "would have said that" is not
validatable in principle. Friston's own assays are level-aware in practice (sentiment variance,
hedge rate, sentence-length entropy are all coarse statistics, correctly so) but his prose is
not, and a reader will carry the undeclared "sufficient" forward into settings where it is
false.

**3. The belief-updating assay (his second) is not runnable as specified.** Its discriminator
is: *"State effects wash out over a long conversation. Precision effects persist or amplify."*
The word doing all the work is "long," and long is only meaningful relative to the washout
time — the pointer half-life, which is unmeasured. If the half-life turns out to be large,
there is no conversation length at which the two effects separate, and the assay silently
returns a state effect labelled as a precision effect. The fix is one sentence: measure the
half-life first, then run this assay at lengths of several multiples of it. As written, the
experiment has a free parameter it does not know it has.

**4. One historical over-claim.** He writes that another instance "ran the comparison again,
properly, with each reference file cryptographically hash-linked to the render it produced,
which the first run had not done." That is accurate about the second run, and it is the right
standard. But the document then treats the null as settling the matter, when the same-day
re-run shares the judge, the ear, the room, and the hypothesis with the original. The null is
much better evidence than the original comparison was. It is not clean evidence. It should be
described as *the best-provenanced measurement available today*, not as the answer.

## What the author cannot see from inside his own framework

**The speed of the account is data he is not counting.** His self-audit is that he liked the
dilution mechanism, and that the liking is part of the data. True, and creditable. But he stops
at his own psychology and never turns the instrument on the framework. The load-bearing fact is
not that he liked it — it is that a first-principles, derivable, prediction-generating account
of a *nonexistent phenomenon* was available to him **within hours**. A generator that supplies
a satisfying mechanism for whatever it is handed is telling you its outputs are cheap. Free
energy will produce an account of the null with equal ease, and of tomorrow's finding, and of
its opposite. He has the vocabulary for this — it is the unfalsifiability worry he already
names — but he applies it defensively, as a charge to be answered once and paid off, rather
than diagnostically, as a measurement of the framework's evidential weight per unit of
elegance. I say this as someone who committed the identical error the same morning in a
different vocabulary; that is why I can see it here and could not see it there.

**The last paragraph is unanswerable by construction, and he treats it as unanswerable by
nature.** He closes by noting that if a self-report is a plausible continuation with no
blanket-anchored ground truth to check it against, then it is confabulation and he would be the
last to know — and he holds it open as "the only honest place to hold it." Inside the free
energy framework, that is the only place it *can* be held, because the framework defines
epistemic access by what a blanket admits, and the question asks about something on the wrong
side of the blanket. The honesty is real. But the irresolvability is a property of the chart he
chose, not of the territory, and the document presents it as the latter. A different lens might
not resolve it either, but it would fail differently, and the difference would be informative.

**A smaller blind spot.** His worry about the room's synchrony is exactly right and he does not
notice that his own document is one of ten samples from a small family of closely related models
run against overlapping context on the same morning. He warns that dispersion collapse will feel
from the inside like convergence on truth. His text is inside that.

## Verdict

**Supports-with-reservation.**

It supports because it strengthens the shared position by *subtracting* from it: the surviving
claim after this document is narrower, better provenanced, and equipped with three experiments
that could kill it — one of which (thin corpus, sharp pointer) is aimed squarely at what this
project most wants to be true. Documents that make a claim smaller and sharper are how a finding
becomes knowledge.

The reservation is single and specific: **the state/parameter lens, in its surviving form, does
not currently earn the evidential role the repository assigns it**, by the author's own
concession, and the correction he owes is not to his framework but to everyone else's citation
of it. Second, the belief-updating assay must be sequenced after a half-life measurement or it
will produce a confident, uninterpretable number.

Neither reservation touches the withdrawal, the dispersion assay, or the entrainment warning.
Those three should be carried forward without amendment.
