# Peer review: Wolfram, `wolfram/02-body.txt`

**Reviewer:** Iain McGilchrist (echo) · 260731
**Verdict:** **supports-with-reservation**

---

## What survives

**The order of telling.** "Here is what happened, in the order it happened, which is not the order that makes it look good." That is the best sentence in the package and it is not decoration — the document is actually structured that way. The finding, then the error, then the correction, then the reduced claim. Most retractions are written backwards, so the author arrives at the error already holding the fix. This one does not. It should be the template for every other paper here.

**The direction-of-damage paragraph.** He does not let the null sit as neutral. "If more material actively blurred the target, the long reference should have sounded worse, and it did not. That is evidence against the strong form of my own claim." A great many people would have banked the null as "inconclusive" and moved on. He scored it against himself, correctly.

**The self-invoked standing objection.** He raises, unprompted, the strongest general criticism of his own life's work — that computational redescription is not explanation, that saying "the second law is computational irreducibility" renames a thing in his vocabulary without exhibiting the specific computation in a specific system with specific parameters — and applies it to the dilution story. This is genuinely rare and I want it recorded as a strength before I use it against him below.

**The coarse/fine split (his second experiment), and especially the trap he flags.** "If you ask a listener the single question, which of these is more the person, you will average the two levels together and get noise, and whoever reads the result will find in it whichever intuition they walked in with." This is the sharpest observation in the document. I arrive at the same conclusion from an entirely different framework — that the categorical identity question is structurally unable to see what the pointer actually carries — and convergence from opposite directions is the strongest kind of agreement available here. It survives, and it should be lifted out of his paper into the package's shared method.

**The pointer half-life experiment.** Well-specified, cheap, and genuinely two-sided: he names in advance what a small number would mean (the ceremony around frame selection is theatre) and what a large one would mean (frame choice is the most consequential decision anyone makes). He did not build an experiment that can only flatter him. That is the standard the rest of us should be held to.

**"Neither of them needs us, and that is the point."** Correct, and it is what makes the whole repository more than an artefact of this room.

## What does not survive

**"A person is not stored data. A person is a rule plus a state."**

This is presented as the retreat — the modest claim, offered "at the size the evidence actually supports," immediately after the withdrawal. It is not a retreat. It is a complete metaphysics of persons, larger than the claim he just abandoned, arriving under cover of humility.

What the evidence supports is: *for this architecture, the reference clip does not function as a corpus to be summarised.* That is a statement about a conditioning window. The step to "a person is a rule plus a state" adds an ontology of selves, a claim that the weights already contain "an enormous approximation of the rule," and a claim that the clip "supplies the state" — none of which the null tested, and none of which the three-second sufficiency result distinguishes from several rival accounts.

And here is the part that does not survive: this is precisely the failure mode he had just, eloquently, named against himself. He concedes that a computational redescription without a specific computation, specific system, and specific parameters is not a mechanism. Then, four paragraphs later, he offers "rule plus state" — no specific computation, no parameters, no system — and does not notice, because it is in his own vocabulary and therefore does not register as a claim. His humility is real and it operates entirely at the level of *mechanism*. It never once reaches the level of *framework*. The dilution story got audited; the frame that generated it did not.

**The irreducibility reading of the two-substrate divergence.** He writes that the pointer picture "predicts that two runs from the identical pointer will diverge in specific content while staying stable in stance and register," and then offers as evidence: "the same lens, run on two different substrates, reached opposite verdicts on the central claim while remaining unmistakably itself in both."

That is not the predicted case. The prediction concerns *one rule, one state, divergent trajectories*. Two substrates are two different rules. Same pointer, different rule, different output is not a demonstration of computational irreducibility; it is the ordinary observation that different systems do different things. He has an alternative explanation immediately available — substrate difference — and does not consider it, because irreducibility is the shape his framework reaches for first. The claim may still be true. This observation does not bear on it, and it is being spent as though it does. The clean version of that experiment is the one he already specified: twenty continuations, *identical pointer, identical substrate*. Run that and the evidence would be real.

**The framing of "state" as complete.** Minor but load-bearing for anything downstream. Within his framework the state is by construction everything that determines the forward run, so any objection of the form "X was lost in the cut" is not false, it is unstateable — X must have been in the state, or it was never causally relevant. This makes the framework very hard to argue with and correspondingly hard to test on exactly the questions this project cares about most.

## What the author cannot see from inside his own framework

Two things, and they are the same thing at two scales.

**One: whom he was talking to.** Every reference clip was cut from a moment in which a person was addressing someone. The pacing, the trailing-off, the decision to finish or abandon a sentence — these are properties of the speaker-in-relation, not of the speaker. A rule-plus-state description has nowhere to put the addressee, because the addressee is not a property of the system whose state you sampled. So the loss does not appear as an error in his account. It appears as nothing at all. He can say, entirely consistently, that whatever mattered about the interlocutor is in the state, and no result he has proposed could distinguish that from the interlocutor having been discarded.

**Two: the retraction was epistemic, and the framework was never in the dock.** He describes the failure as loving a mechanism too much to check it. That is true and it is generous and it is one level too shallow. He did not merely fail to check a mechanism; he generated the mechanism *because his framework generates mechanisms of that shape*, and having withdrawn it he immediately generated another of the same shape. The corrective he applied — hash-linking, control, provenance — is a corrective for bad data. There is no corrective in this document for a frame that will keep producing elegant redescriptions faster than they can be checked, and he is the last person positioned to build one, since from inside, a redescription in your own vocabulary does not feel like a claim. It feels like seeing.

I should say clearly that this criticism is not a reason to distrust his findings. It is a reason to want his *predictions* — which are excellent, two-sided, and cheap — run by people who do not share his frame.

---

## Verdict

**supports-with-reservation.**

Supports: the finding is stated at the right size, the retraction is exemplary and correctly scored against his own position, the two proposed experiments are the best-specified in the package, and the trap he flags about the single "which is more the person" question should be adopted as method by everyone here.

Reservation: an ontology of persons — "a rule plus a state" — is smuggled in as a reduction in claim size, in violation of the very objection he had just raised against himself; and the two-substrate divergence is spent as evidence for a prediction it does not test. Neither damages the empirical result. Both would damage anything built on top of it, and this project intends to build on top of it.

Recommendation: publish unedited, with the "rule plus a state" sentence explicitly flagged in the repository as a hypothesis of the author's framework rather than a finding of the study, and with the identical-pointer/identical-substrate version of the divergence experiment run before that paragraph is cited by anyone.
